A quick and dirty cheat to get the same look as the image in the post, is to open that image in photoshop, then do image - adjust - match color. Choose the post image in the source dropdown, and you will get similar color tones, though the brightness and contrast may not match. By the way, whatever sample image you use, might be a good idea to convert it to a smart layer before going further, so your changes can be easily tweaked or removed.

The proper way to do it is with color grading. ctrl+shift+A to get into Adobe Camera Raw. Normally this is used on raw images but it works fine on jpegs too. Under the color grading tab, try clicking around in the color wheels. Make yellow highlights, maybe green or cyan midtones, blue shadows. The closer you click to the center, the less severe the color toning will be.

![trick-brush-very-limited-flow-4-gradually-paint-while-smoothly-blending-into-blurred-layer-need-pick-choose-should-sit-front-](https://i.imgur.com/0jtRI8F.png)

![trick-brush-very-limited-flow-4-gradually-paint-while-smoothly-blending-into-blurred-layer-need-pick-choose-should-sit-front-](https://i.imgur.com/0jtRI8F.png)

The trick is to use that brush at very limited flow, like 4%, and gradually paint in the details you want, while smoothly blending into the blurred layer. You will need to pick and choose which details should sit in front of the blur, like individual cars and people. It will be a bit tedious. Maybe it's possible to use some smart-select tool to grab that stuff but I don't know. A super advanced option might be to feed the original image to AI and ask it to create a depth map. A depth map is a black and white image representing which parts of an image are closest to the camera. With a depth map you can get a sort of blocky black and white representation of individual objects, like the cars and light poles, and use to make easier selections with e.g. the magic wand. Then you can more easily mask out the details you want to stay on top of the blur.
